## Trade mark registration overview

The Trade Marks Act, 1999 provides for registration and protection of marks that distinguish the goods or services of one person from those of others. A trade mark may include words, names, devices, labels, shapes, packaging, or combinations capable of graphical representation and distinguishing goods or services.

For product-routing purposes, trade mark protection concerns the commercial source identifier or brand, rather than whether the underlying formulation is a patentable invention. The Act contains absolute and relative grounds on which registration may be refused; an application requires an assessment against the applicable statutory requirements and existing marks.

This corpus entry is a high-level retrieval aid, not a registration opinion or clearance search. Consult the official register and qualified advice before adopting or filing a mark.
